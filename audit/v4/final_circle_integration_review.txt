CIRCLE PROOF: INDEPENDENT CHECKS
1. Quantifiers and finite input.
   A target sequence is fixed before choosing a point. For every epsilon the
   finite extraction parameters are fixed before the infinite visit set. The
   displayed threshold powers make theta, epsilon_F and eta tend to zero with
   u, while k and r first control the remaining two exponent terms. The finite
   bound counts every admissible word and is uniform over ALL affine shifts.
   The selected anchor is an actual visit; some rational slopes may be <1.
   The old input requires exactly the positive rational 3-adic units obtained.

2. Periodic real transfer, circle:lem:transfer.
   Z+K01 is closed by local finiteness, so the bounded intersection is compact.
   A K01 tail after L digits lies in [0,3^(-L)/2]; flooring therefore reads the
   exact allowed prefix, including terminating expansions and negative points.
   Independently recomputing the two forms of delta gives
     delta=V-cT-D=ct+theta-t_i in q^(-1)Z intersect (-1,c+1).
   For c=p/q>0 the number of possible errors is p+2q-1, with open endpoints.
   Since 3 does not divide q, D+delta is 3-adically integral. Reduction of
     cU+(D+delta)=V-c(T-U)
   is exact modulo 3^L. At any fixed depth D is fixed; it can change with depth
   without cost because the finite estimate is uniform in the shifts. The
   proof never assumes a depth-independent shift vector. Irrational d_i and
   negative/unbounded fixed shifts cause no additional error choices.
   The resulting cell count has a depth-independent prefactor. Consecutive
   ternary scales suffice for upper box dimension.

3. Packing dimension and countability, circle:main.
   Finite index tuples and finite integer template data form a countable set.
   With the target sequence fixed, their real shifts are determined, rather
   than independent uncountable choices. The unit remainders are determined
   by the slopes and template coefficients. The inverse affine IMAGE of F
   contains the original point and retains its upper box bound. Packing
   dimension is then bounded via a countable compact cover. This argument
   does not incorrectly pass upper box dimension through a countable union.
   In the 3-adic case the normalizing affine map is an isometry and its shift
   vector is integral. The same finite input gives the required compact cover.

4. Target normalization, circle:cor:dimension.
   K01 uses digits 0 and 1; K02=2K01 is the standard middle-thirds target.
   For a K02 visiting point x, put y=x/2. Writing 2^n x=a+2z shows that
   2^n y lies in Z+K01 or Z+1/2+K01 according to a's parity. One alternative
   occurs infinitely often. These are precisely the fixed shifts 0 and -1/2
   in the theorem. Similarity and circle projection yield dimension zero.
   Endpoint identification does not invalidate the inclusion. This is an
   explicit proof of the additional target variant, not an implicit scaling
   assertion about modulo-one dynamics.

5. Finite alternatives and boundary of the conclusion.
   A fixed finite number of target sequences permits the infinite pigeonhole
   argument. An unbounded number of alternatives is not asserted. Nor is a
   union over all target sequences asserted. Dense dyadic rational points
   give both box dimensions one, consistently with packing dimension zero.
   The class of 1 is zero on the circle. No exclusion of the ordinary 3-adic
   starting point 1, no countability theorem and no circle finite-visit
   Assouad theorem is claimed.
