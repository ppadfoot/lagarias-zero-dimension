1. TARGET LANGUAGE AND KNEser CAPACITY (S16 159–227)

A proper closed forward-shift-invariant Y excludes a finite cylinder. That word is excluded at every position. The block bound N_h <= (p^a-1)^floor(h/a) p^(h mod a) is correct. If 2(p^a-1)^t0 < p^(a t0), the ratio 2N_h/p^h remains <1 for all h >= a t0, yielding the claimed h0=a t0-1.

A coset of the subgroup of order p^h modulo p^k fixes the lower k-h digits. After subtracting the target translate, the remaining upper h digits of any admissible word belong to Y_h by forward shift invariance. Thus every coset meets each target translate in at most N_h points (S16 183–192).

For T=A_1+...+A_r contained in two translates, its actual stabilizer H has order p^h and a nonempty T contains a complete H-coset. Therefore p^h <=2N_h and h<=h0. In the quotient by H, every partial sum is aperiodic: any period of a partial sum is a period of the final sum. Kneser's aperiodic sum inequality iterates to sum(n_i-1)<=|T|/|H|-1. Since |A_i|<=|H|n_i and n<=2^(n-1),
 sum log|A_i| <= r h0 log p + 2N_k log2.
This verifies the exact constant and its crucial lack of k in the coefficient of r.

For p=3,Y=Sigma, h0=1 is valid because 3^h>2^(h+1) for h>=2. Thus B_rk=r log3+2^(k+1)log2 and the final bound is 1/k+2^(k+1)log2/(r k log3).

2. COUNTABLE COVER AND NORMALIZATION (S16 269–300, 462–492)

For fixed r,q,K, the chosen true visiting indices are finite and the finite tail approximations A_t,e_ti,E_i are integers. Even though the intermediate limits rho,a_t can range over uncountably many p-adic values, they do not index the final cover. The normalized coefficients c_i and translations d_i are fixed by the original countable family and the finite chosen indices. The covering compacts
 K_D={X in Y: c_iX+d_i in Y for 1<=i<=K}
are indeed compact: they are a finite intersection of closed subsets of the compact Y. Their inverse affine images are compact similarities in Q_p. It is legitimate to use a different countable cover for each fixed set of global parameters.

This report does not independently certify the full ESS extraction, which is assigned to the arithmetic referee. Given the extraction described in S16, there is no residual uncountable-union problem.

For the intended specialization alpha_n=2^n, beta_n=0, target Sigma, the normalizing map is X=2^n0 x; d_i=0 and c_i=2^(ni-n0). Negative exponent differences are harmless because 2 is a 3-adic unit. The tail remainders u_i in c_i=1+sum A_t 3^e_ti+u_i3^E_i need only be 3-adic units, not ordinary integers.

3. FINITE WORD SET W_L (S16 391–397)

It is correct to take every input word whose input and each separately computed output prefix belong to Y_L. A word in W_L need not have one simultaneous infinite continuation satisfying all constraints. The residue c_i X+d_i mod p^L is well defined from X mod p^L because c_i,d_i are p-adic integers. Each output prefix has some continuation in Y by its membership in Y_L, which implies that each internal q-block belongs to Y_q. This separate continuation is all the finite causal relation requires. The true compact K_D has at most |W_L| occupied level-L cylinders, so counting the larger W_L only strengthens the safety of the upper bound.

4. PROJECTED CONDITIONAL SUPPORTS (S16 399–421)

For a realized X and F, C_j is the support of the high k-block conditional on X_<j+b,F. This fixes the low b digits of the X-window and of every auxiliary A_t X-window. Because A_t is a p-adic unit, the map from the next k input digits to the corresponding next k digits of A_t X is an affine bijection modulo p^k with carry determined by the already fixed lower input prefix. Therefore each required row projection has exactly |C_j| possibilities. Additional multiplication of the first role by u_i is also an affine bijection after fixing its lower b digits.

The complete joint q-window lifts of C_j belong to the assigned A_j: conditioning on X_<j+b,F refines conditioning on X_<j,F, and A_j depends only on the latter pair. For a good positional edge, the container lemma gives a full Cartesian product of these A_j for ONE realized input and its fingerprint, with the relation frozen at that realized source prefix. We may therefore select projected supports independently at the endpoints, even though their conditioning prefixes differ and the counterfactual choices might not come from any single global input. The full-product statement is precisely what makes this legal. Merely checking the actual tuples would not suffice.

The lower b digits are fixed separately in the selected endpoint supports, hence the two-target lemma applies. This justifies the pointwise inequality sum_roles log|C_j|<=B_rk. No assumption of probabilistic independence of windows is used.

5. INCIDENCE AND BOUNDARY (S16 424–436)

For a template with span E_i on N starts, each of its r roles misses min(E_i,N) positions. The total weighted deficit is at most r k log p sum_i min(E_i,N), hence at most r k log p K R. This formula remains valid when N<E_i and there are no edges of that template. Counting at most KN edges, giving good edges bound B_rk and bad edges the safe extra r k log p, yields
 rK sum_j w_j <= B_rk KN + r k log p E_bad + r k log p KR.
The displayed division by rK is correct. There is no omitted factor of r or K and no need to assume B_rk<=r k log p.

6. OVERLAPPING ENTROPY (S16 438–459)

Let h_t=H(X_t|X_<t,F), an unconditional nonnegative number. For each high k-block, the entropy chain rule with the same F yields exactly the sum of its h_t. This is bounded by E log|C_j|. Summing N blocks produces total multiplicity kN, whereas full k-fold coverage of all L coordinates has multiplicity kL. Since h_t<=log p, the missing multiplicity is at most k(L-N)log p=k(b+k-1)log p. Hence
 H(X|F)<= (1/k)sum_j E w_j+(b+k-1)log p.
Since F is a deterministic function of X, H(X)=H(F)+H(X|F). Uniform X on W_L gives H(X)=log|W_L|. Combining the previously proved inequalities gives exactly S16 (eq:entropy), including all coefficients.

7. LIMITS AND PACKING DIMENSION (S16 462–492)

For each fixed k,r choose b,q,Q,C,D_c. The power hierarchy T_j=u^(2^(j-1)-1), T_s=u^(2^(r-1)-1), K=u^(2^r) makes every ratio in theta tend to zero; theta<=1/2 eventually, epsilon_F tends to zero, and eta tends to zero. Fix u before the witness systems and before L tends to infinity.

For each such finite system, R is a fixed finite number. Dividing log|W_L| by L log p and letting L→infinity gives an upper box dimension bound. Packing dimension is at most upper box dimension for each compact and is countably stable. Similarities preserve it. Thus taking the countable union of finite witnesses gives the same packing bound on all E. Let u→infinity, then r→infinity at fixed k, then k→infinity. All three transitions are legitimate. Uniformity in R across the countable family is unnecessary. One must not replace packing dimension by upper box dimension of the union, which can be large.

This proves the implication from the finite-container/counting estimates to BOTH dimensions zero. A paper can safely state the packing conclusion if the remaining proof components pass. There is no reason to weaken the final statement to Hausdorff alone as a way to avoid the container issue: both conclusions rely on that same central lemma.

8. ASSOUAD EXTENSION (S17 1217–1288)

Conditionally on the same finite estimate, the additional compact-cover assertion is correct. In a cylinder X=P+p^mU, forward shift invariance gives a subset of another finite system with identical slopes, templates, and span R, but translated centers d_i^(P). The counting estimate is uniform in these centers, because the abstract finite theorem is uniform in arbitrary causal relations. Its multiplicative covering constant therefore does not depend on P,m. This proves dim_A K_D<=s_D. Choosing global parameters gives countable epsilon-covers.

It does not give dim_A E_2=0 because Assouad dimension is not countably stable. The manuscript explicitly avoids that error. Its observation E_2≠{0} => dim_A E_2=1 is consistent: the two-sided 2-orbit of a nonzero exceptional point is dense in its valuation annulus. The sparse factorial Cantor example with dim_A=0 and uncountable cardinality is also correct.

This extension need not be included in the intended focused article. Excluding it reduces scope without changing the packing proof.

9. INDEPENDENT APPENDIX READING (S16 822–1288)

I checked the complete source-dependent construction, not only its application. In particular:
- Complete origins use one actual source letter only after that source is recorded; light source candidates are handled separately and explicitly charged by Q T_s N. Thus the actual-source slice does not substitute for a full source rectangle.
- The pair bound C Q^(r-3) counts fixed full origins over time, so merging old lower edges does not create unlimited fresh charges.
- At each removal active degrees are bounded by thresholds; a physical insertion is charged to its first removed endpoint.
- Rank-j packets can nest only strictly smaller ranks. Selected own origins cannot remain stranded at closure because their frozen rank bounds are below j.
- A rank-j loss is charged to a pair (pivot,excluded literal) within a same-rank time-disjoint packet. Surviving projections are charged through a retained pair to the unique lower-edge insertion event.
- A heavy source's selected future tuples are position-disjoint, so exclusion charges are bounded. A recorded-source bad edge survives until an explicitly charged chronological retirement.
- Replay answers a produced equality query from membership in final F. If an unrecorded source belongs to final F its actual letter must be heavy: the light branch creates no events and immediately retires it, so a future recording would be impossible. This validates the source branch without access to an unrecorded future value.
- All assigned palettes remain fixed after retirement, and no later operation refers to a retired position.
No contradiction was found in this independent reading. This is corroboration; the separate container referee report should be retained alongside it.
