1. EXACT HYPOTHESES AND CONCLUSION
The abstract letters Z_i(x) may be arbitrary functions of the whole finite
input. Relations alone must be causal: R_e depends on x_<s, where s is the
first position of e. Future position sets of edges sharing a source are
disjoint, and every positional pair belongs to at most C edges.
For fixed r and Q, D=C Q^(r-3). The thresholds control two separate losses:
a small set F of true position/letter pairs and O(N) bad positional edges.
For every other edge, the conclusion is a full product of assigned palettes,
not merely compatibility of the one realized tuple.

This distinction is essential. For W={000,001,111} and the ternary sum
condition, successive conditional supports need not have compatible product.
That old negative control does not contradict Appendix A: its additional
fingerprint/palette construction is the step that supplies the missing
product property.

2. DISTINCT FULL ORIGINS, NOT HISTORICAL PROJECTIONS (lines 900–931)
For a fixed input, the source letter of each complete origin is its one true
letter. Prescribing two future literals leaves r-3 future letters free, and
there are at most C original positional edges containing their positions.
Consequently no pair of future literals belongs to more than D distinct
complete origins over the entire run. This count does not grow when an origin
is projected or several origin labels are merged on one physical edge.
Source alternatives are not silently omitted: recorded sources later have
singleton assigned alphabet, whereas light sources pay a separate Q-factor
in the bad-edge estimate. Thus the sharper exponent r-3 is justified.

3. STATE, SOUNDNESS AND DEGREE CONTROL (lines 936–1040)
An origin has one owner and one descending chain of projections. Only recorded
true literals may be substituted. A live projection therefore still contains
a false literal. In particular an empty projection is impossible and a unit
projection safely excludes its sole literal.
Recording a true pivot first snapshots all incident ranks. Rank-two neighbors
are false and are synchronously excluded before any physical insertion.
Every other incident edge becomes a queued projection with lower frozen rank.
The same origin cannot branch during these transfers.

Before an insertion all active degrees are strictly below threshold. An
insertion adds one edge; querying a threshold-reaching endpoint removes that
very new edge immediately, whether the answer is positive or negative. Every
other newly threshold-reaching endpoint therefore falls back below threshold.
No simultaneous insertion is allowed during these removals. Degree control
in all ranks follows by induction.

4. SCHEDULER AND ALL-LIFETIME CHARGES (lines 1017–1166)
A rank-j positive packet drains precisely the queued jobs of frozen rank <j.
Its selected own origins start at frozen rank j-1. Their subsequent frozen
ranks can only decrease. At closure they cannot remain deferred. There is no
chronological retirement during a packet. Hence each selected origin has
exactly the needed exhaustive alternative: a required literal has actually
been excluded during the packet, or the origin is on a live edge of rank <j.

For palette losses, the pivot and excluded literal form a pair in the full
origin, so one exclusion pays at most D invoices in one packet. Same-rank
packets are disjoint in time; the total is at most D QN.
For surviving lower edges, any pair in that lower edge lies in every charged
full origin. Each origin can be selected only once at rank j, because after
selection its arity is permanently below j. Thus one lower-edge insertion
pays at most D invoices over all rank-j packets, even if later origins merge
onto an old edge. This is an all-lifetime bound, not an instantaneous one.

A physical insertion is charged to the first deactivation of one of its ends.
That endpoint is removed once and has degree at most T_j at that moment.
Therefore I_j<=T_j QN. A removed literal is never reactivated, so a physical
edge cannot disappear and reappear with the same literal set.
The resulting bounds are
 H_2 T_2 <= QN,
 H_j T_j <= D(QN + sum_{2<=l<j} I_l),
 H_s T_s <= QN + D sum_j I_j.
For the source bound, its selected outgoing future tuples are disjoint in
positions, so one actual palette exclusion pays at most one invoice for that
source. Source intervals are disjoint in time. These arguments give the exact
stated theta with no additional N-dependent or K-dependent constant.

5. BAD-EDGE BUDGET REALLY COVERS FULL PRODUCTS (lines 1197–1225)
At a light source each assigned source candidate has fewer than T_s outgoing
edges admitting any forbidden tuple in the current future palettes. Assigned
future alphabets can only be smaller. Taking the union over at most Q source
candidates gives at most Q T_s bad outgoing edges per light source.

For a bad edge with recorded source choose one forbidden tuple from the final
assigned alphabets. Its source is the recorded true source. Every future
letter of that tuple survives in its own final assigned alphabet and so could
not have suffered a palette exclusion. Following its unique origin chain,
unit exclusion and empty projection are impossible. With queues empty at
chronological barriers, the chain must terminate on a physical edge at a
chronological retirement. Charging that insertion pays at most D distinct bad
positional edges, by the full-origin pair bound. Thus retirement is explicitly
paid for and E_bad<=Q(T_s+D sum T_j)N follows.

6. REPLAY AND CONDITIONAL SUPPORTS (lines 1230–1293)
A query answer is positive exactly when the queried pair is in final F. This
is an equivalence for queries actually made by the deterministic algorithm;
F is not used to preactivate future recorded literals or to answer queries
that were never asked.
The source branch is also reconstructible. A previously unrecorded source
absent from dom F must be light. A previously unrecorded source present in
F must be heavy: otherwise its event-free light branch would immediately
retire it and it could never be recorded later. This eliminates the sole
other dependence on an unrevealed true letter.

All relations needed through source i depend only on restrictions of x_<i.
Thus the execution through assigning A_i is determined by (x_<i,F), even if
Z_i itself depends on input coordinates far later than i. Taking all inputs
on the same conditioning atom proves
 supp(Z_i | x_<i,F) subset A_i.
No discovery order needs to be encoded in F. At most one true pair is recorded
per position. The fingerprint count is therefore sum_{a<=theta N} binom(N,a)Q^a,
and the usual binomial entropy inequality for theta<=1/2 gives the stated
H(F)<=N[h(theta)+theta log Q].

7. APPLICABILITY TO THE MAIN MANUSCRIPT'S WINDOW SUPPORTS
The abstract no-locality statement is strong enough for joint overlapping
q-windows (X,A_1 X,...,A_{r-2}X) labelled at their left endpoints. One must not
replace its proof by a theorem that requires the whole Z_i to be known at i.
Once a realized x and F are fixed, every later, more finely conditioned window
support is a subset of the A_i determined by that x's shorter prefix. On a
good edge, the product of all such subsets is permitted even when selections
from different factors do not come from one jointly realizable input. This
is precisely what the full-product conclusion supplies. I see no additional
independence assumption at lines 411–416.
