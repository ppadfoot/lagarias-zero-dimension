1. Carry graph and dimension. The complete reachable state set lies in
0<=2c<M; every state has at least one outgoing edge. A branching strongly
connected component gives two equal-length return words with different
first input digits, hence positive dimension. Under zero dimension a
terminal component is a deterministic cycle with a+b=1. Its rational
cycle identity gives the internal real-Cantor witness (2c+1)/(M+1).
Only necessity is used: coexistence of a terminal cycle with another
branching component is not confused with zero dimension.

2. Small orders. The exceptional-prime product, its convergent 9/10
moment, and the squarefull-part 1/2 moment are sufficient as stated.
The product/lcm lower bound for ord_n(3) is valid, including powers of 2.
Outside the three exceptional sets, the remaining radical is at least
X^(9/10)/2 and the resulting exponent 7/20 exceeds 1/3. Dyadic summation
retains O(X/(log X)^10). No GRH assumption enters this argument.

3. External input independently verified. Primary author-hosted PDF:
  https://kurlberg.github.io/eprints/rsa.pdf
Kurlberg--Pomerance, On the periods of the linear congruential and power
generators, Lemma 10, PDF page 7, states exactly the used exceptional-set
bound for Carmichael's function. The following page attributes it to
Theorem 5 of the cited earlier work. I checked the stated lemma in the
primary PDF; I did not reprove that earlier external theorem.

4. Cantor denominators. The rational spacing bound uses level intervals
strictly shorter than T^(-2). Each orbit has exactly ord_d(3) distinct
points and retains its reduced denominator. Thus the orbit count and
the reciprocal tail with kappa=4/3-2 log_3(2)>0 follow. Neither unreduced
representations nor endpoints are included as new denominators.

5. Sieve. Conditional prime-power valuation coordinates are independent
modulo the finite least common multiple, but composite divisibility
events are not. The text correctly applies positive association for
decreasing events. Summability yields both a positive product lower
bound and a uniform tail estimate; these prove a natural density for
the sifted subfamily, not automatically for all positive-dimensional
multipliers.

6. Separation. The finite-witness inclusion M_C subset M_H is proved by
disjoint blocks. The strict quotient intervals leave [3T/2,2T] empty
of M_C for T a power of 3. The density estimate for the translated
sifted set supplies a linear number in this interval for every large X,
not merely a subsequence. The stated lower bound for M_H-M_C follows.
No asymptotic density 1/2, linear lower bound for M_C, or fixed-orbit
finiteness is inferred.
